# Judging and prizes

## Judging process

The Sponsor and Administrator may use expert panels, peer review, automated AI-driven analysis, or a combination. Judges may be Sponsor employees or third parties, may change, and need not all be publicly listed.

### Stage one: viability gate

Pass/fail screening asks whether the project:

- reasonably fits the challenge theme; and
- reasonably applies the required featured API/SDK—in this event, WebMCP.

A polished product that uses WebMCP only superficially can fail here. Conversely, passing this gate is not enough to win; it only advances the entry to scored judging.

### Stage two: scored judging

The four criteria are **equally weighted** and each uses a five-point judging scale.

| Criterion | What judges ask | Evidence to optimise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WebMCP Leverage | How thoroughly and skillfully is WebMCP used? Is the implementation working, non-trivial, and backed by genuine effort? | Multiple useful tools, accurate schemas/descriptions, reliable execution, clear agent/human handoff, code that proves real integration |
| Execution | Is there a working/runnable project with a complete, coherent product experience rather than only a technical proof of concept? | Stable live deployment, polished primary workflow, good errors, consistent demo/repo/site behavior |
| Potential Impact | Does the project make a credible and specific case for a real audience and problem, and does the demonstrated solution address it? | Named user, concrete pain, measurable before/after value, demo aligned to that claim |
| Creativity & Ambition | Is the concept novel and meaningfully different from existing ideas? | A workflow made possible or substantially improved by WebMCP, not a generic UI with a tool bolted on |

### Tie-breaking

Ties are broken by comparing the first criterion, then each next criterion in listed order. If entries remain tied across every criterion, the judges vote. This makes WebMCP Leverage the first tie-breaker.

## Practical judging strategy

- Make the WebMCP advantage visible in the first minute of the demo.
- Demonstrate a complete human-plus-agent task, not isolated tool invocations.
- Show that tool schemas, descriptions, state changes, errors, and confirmation boundaries are intentional.
- Keep the strongest workflow reliable and judge-accessible; breadth is not valuable if the main path breaks.
- Make the repository easy to audit, especially the WebMCP registration and the old/new boundary for an existing app.
- Repeat the same specific user/problem/value story across the description, video, live app, and README.

## Prize conditions

- Each project is eligible for one prize.
- Prizes are non-transferable; the Sponsor may substitute an item of equal or greater value.
- Potential winners are not final winners until identity, qualifications, and their role in the submission are verified.
- Prize affidavits and other required forms must be returned within ten business days after they are sent.
- Prize delivery is due within 60 days after completed required forms are received.
- For a team, the authorised representative receives the prize and is responsible for allocation. For an organization, payment goes to the organization.
- Individual prize items explicitly capped at three members do not expand to match a larger team.
- Winners bear bank, wire, exchange, tax, reporting, and similar costs. US winners may need a W-9; non-US winners may need a W-8BEN. Withholding may apply.
- The Sponsor may decline to award a prize if there are no eligible entries.
